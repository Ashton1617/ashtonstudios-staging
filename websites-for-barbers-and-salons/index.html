the booking button first. then the prices. then the chair.

barbershops, hair salons, nail bars, beauty rooms, brow and lash studios, tattoo studios. your customers already know how to book — on booksy, fresha, treatwell, or by walking in. the website's job isn't to replace that. it's to catch the person who's just googled 'barber st albans' or 'nails near me', convince them in three seconds, and hand them to your booking system without a single wasted tap.

so the book button is the first thing on the page and it stays on screen as they scroll. it goes straight to your existing booking system — nothing changes for your regulars, nothing new for you to learn. your prices are on the site, in full, because 'prices on request' loses people who'd have happily paid. your hours, your address and a tap-to-navigate map are one thumb away.

then the chair. this is a visual trade: the gallery is the pitch. real cuts, real colour, real sets — shot in your shop, not stock — laid out big enough to judge the work. your rating from your booking platform shown as a fact, with the reviews underneath in the customer's words. your team, by name, so a new customer can ask for the barber whose work they liked.

goodfellas barbershop in st albans is the working example: a shop with a 5.0 rating and a live booksy diary whose old site gave a new customer no way to book or enquire. the new build puts the booking button first, lists every cut and price, shows the reviews, and looks the way the shop feels. the case study explains what was done and why, and you can click through to the build itself.

  • booking link front and centre

    a 'book now' button in the header and pinned on mobile, wired straight to your existing booksy, fresha or treatwell page. your regulars notice nothing; new customers find it instantly.

  • the full price list

    every cut, colour, set and treatment with its price, laid out like a proper menu and kept in step with your booking system.

  • a gallery that sells the chair

    your work, shot in your shop, given the room it deserves. the first thing a new customer judges is the last thing most salon sites show properly.

  • rating and reviews, as facts

    your platform rating shown plainly, with reviews quoted in the customer's words underneath — on your own domain, not just theirs.

  • the team, by name

    each barber or stylist with a photo and what they're known for, so people can ask for the right person.

  • hours, map, one tap

    opening hours that are actually current, an address that opens the maps app, a number that dials.
