st albans · al1 – al4

a cathedral city with expensive taste. your website is judged against that.

st albans is one of the most competitive places in hertfordshire to run a small business, and one of the most rewarding. the market has run on the same streets since the middle ages; george street, holywell hill and london road are lined with independents; the trains put st pancras twenty minutes away, and the people who moved here for that commute have money and standards. they compare barbers, restaurants, dentists and builders the way they compare everything else — on their phones, quickly, and on looks first.

that makes st albans hard on average websites. a barbershop with a five-star booking profile and a 'not secure' warning on its homepage is handing customers to the shop next door. a physio with a great reputation and no online booking is losing the people who wanted to book at eleven at night. a builder whose whole portfolio lives on instagram isn't found when someone searches 'loft conversion st albans'.

goodfellas barbershop is a real example. a long-established st albans shop with a 5.0 rating and a live booksy diary — and a site that gave a new customer no way to book, email or enquire. the build i've done for them puts the booking button first, lists the real cuts and prices, shows the reviews, and looks the way the shop deserves. you can read how in the case study, or click through the build itself.

that's the standard for every st albans business i work with: your booking or enquiry route front and centre, your real prices and real reviews on your own domain, photography treated like it matters, and a site that loads fast on the thameslink platform. preview first, free. the build only happens once you've seen it and want it.
